Data Analysis & Interpretation

The data suggests that the highest concentration of smartphone addiction parameters falls under the "Moderately Addicted" and "Neutral" ranges. Specifically, 37.19% of respondents indicate moderate addiction relating to study habits and academic concentration, meaning they spend longer periods on smartphones than intended and struggle to shorten usage time.

These behaviors are consistent with research by Hooper and Zhou (2007), who associated smartphone usage with behavioral patterns like habitual, dependent, and compulsive use. However, Cassidy (2006) points out that intensive phone interactions can sometimes serve as positive alternatives to other risk-taking behaviors (e.g., alcohol or tobacco consumption).

Data Analysis & Interpretation

A vast majority of the respondents—64.46% (156 students)—achieved "Outstanding" performance, followed by 26.86% (65 students) who achieved "Very Satisfactory" grades. Encouragingly, no student fell below the passing threshold of 75.

Students with outstanding performance typically exhibit high self-discipline, prioritizing studies over social media and mobile gaming. In addition, some outstanding students reported having limited phone ownership, depending primarily on class focus and traditional study routines (Kabir, 2016).

Data Analysis & Interpretation

The overall summary shows that 55.79% (135 students) fall under the "Neutral" category, indicating that while they use smartphones frequently, they maintain control and do not exhibit compulsive technology dependency. Only a small group, 1.24% (3 students), are categorized as "Strongly Addicted," while 18.18% (44 students) are "Moderately Addicted."

According to Sheila and Steve (2020), because smartphone use has become an absolute social norm, a neutral or moderate addiction level is highly common. However, the presence of 19.42% of students showing moderate-to-strong addiction profiles highlights the need for targeted digital wellness interventions.

Data Analysis & Interpretation

With a calculated Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.0345, there is a very weak, close-to-zero positive relationship between smartphone addiction scores and academic GPAs. Furthermore, the calculated p-value is 0.59329, which is significantly greater than the standard alpha level of 0.05.

Therefore, we fail to reject the null hypothesis (H0). The statistical analysis demonstrates that there is no significant relationship between the level of smartphone addiction and the academic performance of Senior High School students at Dumanjug National High School. While some students spend significant time on screen activities, it does not directly predict a decline in their quarterly GPAs. Other factors (e.g., study habits, parent supervision, or personal motivation) may act as confounding variables, as suggested by Ng et al. (2017).
