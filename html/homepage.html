Overview

Abstract

The development of telecom technology has a significant impact on the academic lives of students. Smartphone usage has become highly popular among the younger generation due to its educational and entertaining options via numerous mobile applications. Increasingly, students are relying on smartphones for daily connectivity. However, excessive smartphone usage can make students addicted, negatively impacting their academic performance, daily activities, physical and mental health, and social relationships. This study aims to identify the factors affecting the level of smartphone addiction among students and its direct impact on their academic performance.

A structured questionnaire was developed to gather data from Senior High School students at Dumanjug National High School, resulting in 242 completed surveys. The data was analyzed using Pearson's r correlation. Results revealed key smartphone addiction determinants such as craving, social relationship dependency, physical discomfort, and study habit distractions. Factors like daily life disturbance and impatience were notable, yet the overall correlation coefficient between smartphone addiction and academic performance was found to be minimal (Pearson's r = 0.0345, p-value = 0.59329), indicating no statistically significant direct relationship in this population. The study suggests that students should reduce intense, unstructured smartphone use to maintain healthy daily-life activities and balanced academic focus.
