The purpose of this study is to delve into the complex relationship between smartphone addiction and academic performance among students. It aims to shed light on the factors that contribute to smartphone addiction and to understand how this addiction impacts student academic success. Understanding the determinants of this addiction and its influence on academic performance is crucial for developing effective interventions and promoting effective interventions and responsible digital citizenship.

Statement of the Problem

This study aims to investigate the determinants of smartphone addiction and its impact on the academic performance of Senior High School students at DUMANJUG NATIONAL HIGH SCHOOL.

Specific Questions

The study seeks to answer the following questions:

  1. What is the level of smartphone addiction among the students of Dumanjug National High School?
  2. What is the academic performance of the respondents in the first quarter of the school year?
  3. What is the relationship between the level of smartphone addiction and academic performance?
  4. What are the primary factors that contribute to smartphone addiction among these students?
  5. What strategies or interventions can be recommended to address smartphone addiction and improve academic performance among students?

Scope and Delimitation of the Study

This study focuses on identifying and analyzing the determinants of smartphone addiction among Senior High School students at Dumanjug National High School. The determinants analyzed include: cravings, social relationships (frequency and enjoyment of social communications via phone), behavioral consequences, and physical discomfort.

The research sample is delimited to 242 Senior High School students enrolled at Dumanjug National High School during the school year 2024-2025, selected via random sampling due to time and resource constraints. The study focuses on overall academic performance and does not examine the influence of specific smartphone apps or other variables (e.g., socio-economic status, learning disabilities, or family support).

Research Hypothesis

Null Hypothesis (H0)

There is no significant relationship between smartphone addiction and academic performance among students at Dumanjug National High School.

Alternate Hypothesis (Ha)

Smartphone addiction significantly influences the academic performance of the students at Dumanjug National High School.

Background of the Study

Smartphones are the most wanted gadget in this era and smartphone addiction is associated with its use. (Bugti,2023). According to Nair et al, (2014), smartphones are electronic gadgets mostly used for two-way communication (Bugti,2023). Smartphone addiction is a disorder involving compulsive overuse of mobile devices and/or the total amount of time they are online over a specified time. Compulsive smartphone use is just one type of technology addiction. However, other technologies prone to overuse like social media and gaming, are often accessed through mobile devices (Hanna & Wigmore,2024)

Boumsleh and Jaaloouk (2017) found an apparent association between smartphone use and academic achievement partly explained by the nature of the task the student is engaged in when using a smartphone. Smartphone addiction is increasingly affecting the masses and is negatively impacting the younger generation. However no work has been done to predetermine academic performance from smartphone addiction using data mining techniques (Chaudhury & Tripathy,2018)

There are few studies on smartphone addiction. In a study on smartphone addiction Cho and Kim (2014), identified gender average daily using time in a week, average daily using time in weekend, wrist pain in using smartphone, accident in using smartphone, sociality, impulsiveness, and social networking sites (SNS) addiction as significant predictors. They have found 43.3% explained variance of these factors in smartphone addiction.

While using smartphones people become unmindful, cause thousands of deaths and faulty activities. Its adverse effect is also seen in work-related tasks, classroom learning (Hiscock,2004;SELWYN,2003, and academic performance (Kuss & Griffths,2011). In the classroom, students engage in surfing the web, social networking, checking emails and text messages and consequently pay less attention to their lessons. Moreover, students spend more time with their smartphone that hampers their regular studies.
