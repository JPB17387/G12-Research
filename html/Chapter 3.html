Chapter 3

Research Design and Methodology

This chapter outlines the structural design of our study, the environment in which the research was conducted, our sampling method, survey instrumentation, and statistical calculations.

Research Design Research Environment Sample & Population Research Instrument Data Collection Statistical Treatment

Research Design

The study employed a correlational research design to evaluate and compare two primary variables: smartphone addiction levels (independent variable) and academic performance (dependent variable) of the respondents. By using a correlational model, the study seeks to determine whether a statistical relationship exists between student screen habits and quarterly GPA, and to understand the strength of that connection without direct experimental manipulation.

Research Environment

The study was conducted at Dumanjug National High School, one of the primary public educational institutions in Dumanjug, Cebu. The campus is located in close proximity to Cebu Technological University (CTU) Dumanjug Extension in Barangay Li-ong, Dumanjug, Cebu. The institution comprises approximately 62 faculty members, administrative staff, and the principal, catering to a student body of roughly 2,000 learners. The school's mission is to facilitate quality instruction and promote academic and vocational improvements.

Sample and Population

The total population of Grade 11 and Grade 12 Senior High School students at Dumanjug National High School is 615 students (340 Grade 11 students and 275 Grade 12 students). To determine the ideal sample size, the researchers utilized Slovin's Formula with a 5% margin of error:

\[n = \frac{N}{1 + N \cdot e^2}\]
  • n = Sample size to be determined
  • N = Total population size (615)
  • e = Margin of error (0.05)

Calculation Walkthrough

Following Slovin's equation, the calculation is structured as follows:

  1. Calculate error margin squared: \(e^2 = 0.05^2 = 0.0025\)
  2. Multiply population by error squared: \(615 \cdot 0.0025 = 1.5375\)
  3. Add constant value: \(1 + 1.5375 = 2.5375\)
  4. Divide total population by result: \(n = \frac{615}{2.5375} \approx 242.36\)

Thus, a random sample of 242 Senior High School students was established as the survey group.

Research Instrument

The questions are divided into specific categories of addiction determinants:

  • Craving (4 items): Gauging anxiety and restlessness when the phone is unavailable.
  • Social Relationship Dependency (4 items): Evaluating preference for online vs. face-to-face communications.
  • Physical Discomfort (4 items): Identifying physical consequences (e.g., wrist and neck pain).
  • Study Habit Distractions (5 items): Investigating device triggers and study interruptions.

Data Collection

Surveys were administered face-to-face to Senior High School students on campus. This approach ensured direct engagement, allowing researchers to clarify terms, maintain control over survey distribution, and ensure a high completion rate.

Questionnaires recorded daily usage patterns, preferred applications, and demographic metrics. Crucially, the survey recorded the respondents' first-quarter Grade Point Average (GPA) to serve as the core metric for academic performance. All data collected was treated with absolute confidentiality.

Statistical Treatment of the Data

To determine if a relationship exists between smartphone addiction and academic performance, the researchers calculated Pearson's r correlation coefficient and the corresponding p-value.
